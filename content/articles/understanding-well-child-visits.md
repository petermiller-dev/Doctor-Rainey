A well-child visit is a checkup scheduled when your child is healthy. It is a chance to follow growth and development over time, to catch concerns early, and to talk through the questions that come with each stage of childhood.

## What happens at a well-child visit

While every visit is tailored to your child's age, most include:

- **Growth measurements** — height, weight, and, for young children, head size — tracked over time.
- **A physical examination** from head to toe.
- **Developmental check-ins** on movement, speech, learning, and social skills.
- **Conversation** about sleep, eating, behavior, safety, and school.
- **Immunizations**, when they are due.

Just as important is the time to ask questions. Well-child visits are as much about the conversation as the examination.

## How often are well-child visits recommended?

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends more frequent visits in the first years of life, when children grow and change quickly, and yearly visits after that. The general schedule is:

- **Infancy:** within the first week after birth, then at 1, 2, 4, 6, 9, and 12 months
- **Toddler years:** at 15, 18, 24, and 30 months
- **Age 3 and older:** once a year through adolescence

Your pediatrician may suggest additional visits depending on your child's needs.

## Why visits matter when your child seems well

Many developmental and health concerns are easier to address when they are noticed early, and some are not obvious at home. Regular visits also build a relationship over time, so that when your child is sick, they are seen by someone who already knows them.

## Preparing for a well-child visit

Bring a list of questions, any forms needed for school, sports, or childcare, and an updated list of medications. For older children, encourage them to think of a question or two of their own.
