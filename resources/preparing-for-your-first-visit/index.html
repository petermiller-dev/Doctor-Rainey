Preparing for Your Child's First Visit

A simple guide to preparing for your child's first visit with a new pediatrician — what to bring, what to ask, and how to help your child feel at ease.

Meeting a new pediatrician is an important step for any family. A little preparation helps the first visit feel calm and useful — for your child and for you.

Gather your child's records

If your child has seen another doctor, ask that office to share records before your visit, or bring copies with you. The most helpful items are:

  • Immunization records
  • Notes from recent checkups or specialist visits
  • A list of current medications, vitamins, and supplements
  • Any known allergies

Write down your questions

Parents often remember their most important question on the drive home. Keep a running list on your phone in the days before the visit, and bring it with you. No question is too small.

Help your child know what to expect

For young children, a simple explanation goes a long way: the doctor will say hello, listen to their heart, look in their ears, and check how much they have grown. Reading a picture book about visiting the doctor, or playing "doctor" with a stuffed animal, can make the visit feel familiar.

Older children and teens appreciate being included. Let them know they are welcome to ask their own questions.

On the day

Arrive a few minutes early so there is no rush. Bring a favorite toy or book for little ones, and a snack and drink if the visit falls near a mealtime.

After the visit

Before you leave, make sure you know when your next visit should be and how to reach the office if questions come up later.

This article is general information for parents and is not a substitute for medical advice about your own child. If you are worried about your child's health, contact your pediatrician. In an emergency, call 911.
